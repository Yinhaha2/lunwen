### English draft

The integration of autonomous coding agents into software development has made the pull request (PR) a public unit of agent work, and merge rate has become the default score for whether those agents succeed. For performance-oriented changes, that score is a poor instrument: it collapses reviewed acceptance, low-friction merge, technical rejection, and silent abandonment into a single average. This study conducts an empirical analysis of 1,219 agent-authored performance PRs from 446 open-source repositories, covering five agents (OpenAI Codex, GitHub Copilot, Devin, Cursor, and Claude Code). We attach refreshed GitHub artifacts and apply a schema-constrained large language model (LLM) analysis to label optimization layer, review concern, evidence quality, and an analytic capability-boundary type. Our findings reveal that the terminal merge rate is 56.9% (671/1,180). Merges are dominated by a low-friction path—median lifetime about five minutes, often with no formal review—rather than by measurement-backed acceptance. Closures are dominated by silent abandonment (282/509 = 55.4%) rather than written technical rejection (164/509 = 32.2%). When a closed PR did receive review or a blocking signal (195/509), the leading failures are functional correctness and design. Outcomes stratify by boundary tag: routine technical-stack edits have a terminal merge rate of 79.6%, compared with 35.7% on process-bound PRs and 12.5% on evidence-bound PRs. These results indicate that agent evaluation should report the path to the outcome, not only the outcome.

### English draft

The integration of large language models (LLMs) into software development tools has moved past inline completion. Autonomous coding agents now open pull requests, respond to review, and retry continuous integration (CI) on real repositories. Recent corpora of agent-authored PRs show that this practice is no longer anecdotal: agents submit changes at scale, and maintainers already treat those PRs as part of ordinary review load. Tools such as GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, Cursor, Devin, and OpenAI Codex differ in interface, but they share a new unit of work—the agent-authored PR—whose recorded outcome is merge or close.

That visibility has made merge rate the headline metric for agent work. We argue that this is the wrong headline for performance engineering. A performance PR asks maintainers to believe a claim about runtime, build cost, or resource use. Believing that claim is a different technical and social act from accepting a small refactor. Prior research has extensively studied correctness, security, and privacy of LLM-generated code, and more recent work has examined function-level efficiency on programming benchmarks. Those studies answer whether a snippet is right, safe, or faster on a controlled input. They do not answer how a performance-oriented change behaves once it is a PR: how long it lives, who reads it, what evidence is requested, and why it disappears.

Studies of agentic PRs that stop at acceptance or rejection rates likewise leave the mechanism opaque. We would like to note that a closed PR may be a documented technical veto, an expired stale bot, or an author who abandoned the thread. A merged PR may be a reviewed optimization, or a five-minute merge of a small patch that nobody measured. If merge is treated as quality, agents will be rewarded for patches that never entered a performance-engineering check, and blamed for closures that never received a human sentence.

To fill this gap, we study the outcome landscape of agent-authored performance PRs rather than a single pass rate. Our corpus contains 1,219 performance-related PRs written by five agents in 446 GitHub repositories. We combine GitHub artifacts with a structured LLM analysis that labels optimization layer, review concern, reproducibility of materials, and an analytic capability boundary. In particular, our study aims to answer four research questions (RQs):

RQ1: What is the outcome landscape of agent-authored performance PRs?

We find a terminal merge rate of 56.9% (671/1,180). Agents differ by more than a factor of two. Most merges are low-friction; most closures are silent abandonment rather than written rejection.

RQ2: How do agent-authored performance PRs get merged?

Successful PRs are short-lived and lightly reviewed. When validation is observable, it is dominated by code reading rather than profiling. Absence of formal review appears on both merged and closed PRs.

RQ3: What are the root causes of failed agent-authored performance PRs?

After restricting attention to closures with review or a rejection signal (195/509), the leading failures are functional correctness and design. Process and evidence boundaries stratify outcomes more sharply than inefficiency antipatterns.

RQ4: How can the outcomes of these performance PRs be improved?

Merged and closed PRs separate first by lifetime and boundary type, not by who attached a benchmark table. The data support two paths, and do not support adding a benchmark to every PR.

To facilitate research reproducibility, we make the filtered corpus, scripts, and annotation schema available in our replication package.\footnote{link}

Our contributions are summarized below.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An outcome-aware reading of agent-authored performance PRs.} We report a terminal merge rate of 56.9\% (671/1,180), document large descriptive gaps across agents, and show that both merge and close are internally heterogeneous: 75.6\% of merges follow a low-friction path, and 55.4\% of closures are silent abandonment.

\item \textbf{A mechanism-level account of success and failure.} We show that observable validation is dominated by code reading; that reviewed failures concentrate on correctness and design; and that process and evidence boundaries stratify terminal merge rates from 79.6\% to 12.5\%.

\item \textbf{Path-specific implications for agents and maintainers.} We separate a fast path, on which routine technical-stack edits already merge without measurement, from a slow path, on which missing reproducible evidence and weak in-PR repair keep PRs closed. Adding a benchmark to every performance PR is not supported by the material signals.
\end{itemize}

### 中文翻译

大语言模型进入软件开发工具之后，工作单元已经越过行内补全。自主编程智能体现在会在真实仓库里打开 Pull Request、回应审查并重跑 CI。近年的智能体 PR 语料表明这不再是轶事：智能体已规模化提交改动，维护者也已把这些 PR 当成日常审查负荷的一部分。GitHub Copilot、Claude Code、Cursor、Devin 与 OpenAI Codex 的界面不同，但共享一个新的工作单元——智能体撰写的 PR——其记录结果是合并或关闭。

这种可见性让合并率变成了衡量智能体工作的头条指标。我们认为，对性能工程而言，这是错误的头条。一条性能 PR 要求维护者相信一个关于运行时间、构建成本或资源占用的主张。相信该主张，与接受一次小重构，是不同的技术与社会行为。已有研究广泛考察了 LLM 生成代码的正确性、安全与隐私，较新的工作也在编程基准上考察了函数级效率。它们回答的是片段是否正确、是否安全、在受控输入上是否更快，而不回答一条性能向改动一旦成为 PR 之后会怎样：它活多久、谁读它、要什么证据、又为何消失。

仅停在接受率或拒绝率的智能体 PR 研究同样把机制留在黑箱里。需要说明的是：关闭的 PR 可能是有文档的技术否决，可能是过期的 stale 机器人，也可能是作者放弃了讨论；合并的 PR 可能是经过审查的优化，也可能是一次五分钟内合入、从未被测量过的小补丁。若把合并当成质量，智能体将因从未进入性能工程核查的补丁而受奖，也会因从未收到一句人类评论的关闭而受责。

为填补这一缺口，我们研究智能体性能 PR 的结果图景，而不是单一通过率。语料包含五类智能体在 446 个 GitHub 仓库中撰写的 1,219 条性能相关 PR。我们将 GitHub 附属数据与结构化 LLM 分析结合，标注优化层面、审查关注点、材料可复现性与分析性能力边界。具体而言，本研究试图回答四个问题：

RQ1：智能体性能 PR 的结果图景是什么？终态合并率 56.9%（671/1,180）；不同智能体相差一倍以上；多数合入是低摩擦，多数关闭是沉默遗弃。

RQ2：这些 PR 如何被合入？成功 PR 短命且审查轻；可观测验证以读码为主；无人审同时出现在合入与关闭两侧。

RQ3：失败的根因是什么？限制在有审查或否决信号的关闭（195/509）后，主导失败是正确性与设计；流程与证据边界比反模式更能分层结果。

RQ4：如何改善这些性能 PR 的结局？合入与关闭首先由寿命和边界类型分开，而不是由谁附了 benchmark 表。数据支持两条路径，不支持给每条 PR 加表。

为便于复现，我们将在复制包中公开筛选后的语料、脚本与标注 schema。

本文贡献如下。

- **对智能体性能 PR 的结果感知式阅读。** 终态合并率 56.9%（671/1,180）；Agent 之间存在大的描述性差异；合入与关闭在内部都是异质的：75.6% 的合入走低摩擦路径，55.4% 的关闭是沉默遗弃。
- **对成功与失败的机制层说明。** 可观测验证以读码为主；被审失败集中在正确性与设计；流程与证据边界把终态合并率从 79.6% 分到 12.5%。
- **对智能体与维护者的分路径含义。** 区分已经能在无测量情况下合入的技术栈快路径，以及因缺少可复现证据、同 PR 修复弱而保持关闭的慢路径。给每条性能 PR 加 benchmark 并不被材料信号支持。

### English draft

In this section, we distinguish AI-assisted from agentic software engineering, introduce the five agents in our corpus, and present a motivating example.

### Agentic Software Engineering

AI-assisted software engineering places a model next to a human editor: the developer remains the author of the commit. Agentic software engineering shifts authorship. An agent plans a change, edits files, runs tests or linters, opens a pull request, and sometimes replies to review. The artifact that others must judge is no longer a suggested snippet but a PR, with a title, a body, a diff, CI, and a recorded outcome. For an assistant, “performance” may mean suggestion acceptance. For an agent, the visible outcome of the work is whether the PR is merged or closed—and, for a performance PR, whether anyone checked the claimed gain.

### Autonomous Coding Agent in Software Engineering

Our corpus attributes PRs to five agents using the agent field in the source dataset. OpenAI Codex accounts for 639 of 1,219 PRs. GitHub Copilot accounts for 222, Devin for 225, Cursor for 95, and Claude Code for 38. We treat these names as corpus identities, not as a controlled comparison of underlying models. Repository mix, task shape, and patch size travel with the label.

### An example of performance challenges in agent-authored PRs

Consider three performance PRs that would look similar in a merge-rate table and unlike one another in review.

On the merge side, Codex pull request 1377 in \texttt{MontrealAI/AGI-Alpha-Agent-v0} proposes a localized speed-up of a Pareto-front routine. The recorded outcome reason is a small-scope, low-risk merge. The public thread does not contain a benchmark table. Under merge rate the PR is a success; under performance engineering it is a non-event: nobody had to be convinced with numbers.

On the close side, Cursor pull request 10623 in \texttt{vercel/turborepo} proposes faster hashing for a manual path. A maintainer closes the PR after stating that real benchmarking is required. The outcome is also a non-merge, but the mechanism is the opposite of silence: the project applied a measurement standard that the submission did not meet. A third case, Claude Code pull request 3375 in \texttt{zenml-io/zenml}, is closed after \texttt{CHANGES\_REQUESTED} because the patch pickles data instead of using the project’s materializers. The blocker is design fit, not a missing loop idiom.

These three endings—fast merge without measurement, close for missing evidence, and close for the wrong abstraction—are what a single merge rate cannot distinguish. The rest of this paper measures how common each ending is.

### English draft

In this section, we present the corpus and the analysis setup. In particular, we describe how we obtain 1,219 agent-authored performance PRs and how we produce structured labels for the research questions.

### Corpus construction

We start from an expanded snapshot of agent-authored pull requests in the AIDev ecosystem. A PR is retained if it is performance-related under at least one of two detectors: an LLM title-and-body classifier (1,158 PRs) or an AIDev performance-task tag (`aidev_pr_task_type_perf_only`, 61 PRs). After removing deleted GitHub objects, the union is 1,219 PRs in 446 repositories. We do not require the AIDev task type to be `perf`: many performance-oriented changes are filed as `feat` (526) or `fix` (198). Restricting to the `perf` tag would measure naming conventions, not performance work.

The overview of the corpus is shown in Table~\ref{tab:corpus}. A PR is \emph{merged} when a merge timestamp is recorded (671). It is \emph{open} when it is still open at the analysis snapshot (39). Every other terminal PR is \emph{closed} (509). Closed means not merged. It does not mean that a maintainer wrote a rejection. Unless a research question says otherwise, comparative rates use the terminal set (merged + closed, $n=1{,}180$). The terminal merge rate is 56.9\% (671/1,180).

### Experimental setup

Figure~\ref{fig:overview} shows the four steps of data collection and labeling.

\textbf{Step 1. Collecting agent-authored PRs.} The unit of analysis is a GitHub pull request, keyed by the global PR id, with author identity in the five-agent field.

\textbf{Step 2. Filtering performance-related PRs.} We apply the two detectors above and keep the union. Each master row stores agent, timestamps, AIDev task type, BERTopic assignment, and detection source.

\textbf{Step 3. Refreshing GitHub artifacts.} We re-query GitHub, remove 404 objects, and write commits, file-level diffs, reviews, issue comments, inline comments, timelines, and linked issues. Formal reviews exist for only a minority of PRs. Missing artifacts are treated as missing, not as evidence that a concern was absent.

\textbf{Step 4. Analyzing PR data.} For each PR we assemble a bounded payload (metadata, body, reviews, comments, commit messages, file statistics, timeline; patches truncated) and prompt an LLM with a fixed protocol, a JSON schema, and six human-authored few-shot analyses. The model returns quantitative metrics, performance labels (optimization layer, antipattern, outcome reason, detection method, reproducibility, regression handling, boundary tag), and a short structured narrative. We aggregate the JSON into the table used by the research questions. Labels such as \code{outcome\_reason} and \code{boundary\_tag} are analytic constructs, not GitHub close reasons. GitHub status remains the outcome variable.

We do not execute the patches. This study measures the public process of performance-oriented agent PRs. It does not measure whether a claimed speed-up would reproduce under a profiler.

### 6.1 RQ1: What does the outcome landscape of agent-authored performance PRs?

**书写思路（中文）。** 只画版图：总体 + Agent；merged 内部；closed 内部。纠正两个误读：合入 ≠ 高质量审查通过；关闭 ≠ 被审过并否决。Agent 差异只描述。

**配图意见。** Fig：状态分布；五类 Agent 终态合并率（参考线 56.9%）；closed 四类机制。Table：Agent 锁定表；可选 optimization_layer Top。寿命图留给 RQ2。

#### English draft

\noindent\textbf{Motivation:}
If merge rate is the score, we first need to know what the score is averaging. Treating “closed” as “rejected by a reviewer” would invent a quality gate that many threads never contained. Treating “merged” as “reviewed and measured” would invent a performance-engineering process that most successes never entered. RQ1 maps the landscape before any path is read as skill.

\noindent\textbf{Approach:}
We report GitHub status on the full corpus ($n=1{,}219$). Comparative merge rates use terminal PRs ($n=1{,}180$). Agent rates are shown for every agent; Claude Code ($n=38$) is not ranked as a model comparison. Internal structure of merged and closed PRs uses the labels in the official RQ analysis. For closures we apply a mechanism partition: \emph{real rejection} (blocking review, \texttt{CHANGES\_REQUESTED}, or an explicit technical or design veto), \emph{silent abandonment} (stale, no review, author walk-away, or bot expiry), \emph{other process}, and \emph{unclear}. Open PRs enter only the corpus-level counts.

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
\textbf{The terminal merge rate is 56.9\% (671/1,180), but merged and closed are internally heterogeneous.}
As shown in the status distribution, 671 PRs are merged (55.0\% of 1,219), 509 are closed (41.8\%), and 39 remain open (3.2\%). The corresponding terminal reject rate is 43.1\% (509/1,180).

Agents do not share one rate. As shown in the agent table, OpenAI Codex has a terminal merge rate of 71.9\% (452/629). Cursor has 54.5\% (48/88) and Claude Code has 61.8\% (21/34, $n=38$ in the corpus). Copilot and Devin sit lower: 37.7\% (77/204) and 32.4\% (73/225). We report these gaps as descriptive facts. They may mix model behavior with repository choice, default patch size, and whether the agent opens unsolicited optimizations. They are not a leaderboard.

The work sits in ordinary product layers. The most common optimization layers are application service (201/1,219 = 16.5\%), build (165 = 13.5\%), frontend UI (137 = 11.2\%), and runtime library (122 = 10.0\%). Each compiler-related layer is under 4\%.

Merged is not one path. Of 671 merges, 507 (75.6\%) are low-friction fast merges, 142 (21.2\%) merge after review iteration, and 22 (3.3\%) merge without formal review but are not fast. Among merged PRs the median lifetime is 0.077 hours (about five minutes); 528 (78.7\%) are tagged \code{fast\_merge}; 391 (58.3\%) have zero comments; 450 (67.1\%) have no formal review. Across the corpus, 1,004 of 1,219 PRs (82.4\%) have no linked issue. Reading merge as “the optimization survived review” overstates review depth for the majority class.

Closed is not one path either. Among 509 closures, silent abandonment accounts for 282 (55.4\%) and real rejection for 164 (32.2\%); other process accounts for 26 (5.1\%) and unclear for 37 (7.3\%). Inside abandonment, the author closes or walks away in 100 cases, the PR is closed without review engagement in 80, long inactivity accounts for 64, and bot expiry accounts for 38. Only 89 closed PRs have \code{blocking=true}. The reviewed-or-rejected subset is 195 PRs (38.3\% of closed). Most non-merges never become a written argument about performance.

\begin{tcolorbox}
\textbf{Finding 1.} The terminal merge rate is 56.9\% (671/1,180), but the average hides two majorities: 75.6\% of merges are low-friction, and 55.4\% of closures are silent abandonment.
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{center}

### 6.2 RQ2: How do agent-authored performance PRs get merged?

**书写思路（中文）。** RQ2.1 画像成功；RQ2.2 放行依据与“不审”。Closed 更常带数字声称和 benchmark 表，不能写“成功因为材料更好”。变更分箱用 RQ 报告：≤100 为 **489 / 61.6%**。

#### English draft

\noindent\textbf{Motivation:}
RQ1 showed that most merges are short and lightly reviewed. That fact can be read as a cheap quality gate (the patch is small, readable, and CI-quiet) or as an attention mismatch (the PR never entered a human queue). RQ2 asks what successful PRs look like, what maintainers can be observed to use as a pass, and whether the absence of review behaves like a gate.

\noindent\textbf{Approach:}
Comparisons use terminal PRs only ($n=1{,}180$). We bin lifetime and churn and report merge rates inside each bin. We compare medians of merged versus closed PRs. Maintainer validation is read from the multi-label field \code{detection\_method} and from body-level evidence signals. We cross those signals with \code{boundary\_tag}. We do not interpret a higher comment count as deeper review.

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
\textbf{Successful performance PRs are fast, slightly smaller, and lightly reviewed; measurement is rarely the pass.}
As shown in the lifetime bins, the terminal merge rate is 76.6\% under one hour (573 PRs), 57.2\% between one hour and one day (236), 40.0\% between one and seven days (180), and 16.7\% after seven days (150). Absence of review is therefore common on both sides. \textbf{Finding 2.} The merge path is a fast, low-attention regime: the terminal merge rate is 76.6\% under one hour and 16.7\% after seven days, and observable validation is dominated by code reading rather than measurement.
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{center}

**书写思路（中文）。** 标题沿用导师原文，正文立刻收窄：不是 509 条 closed 的根因，而是被审 / 有否决信号的 **195** 条。反模式只作负对照。`antipattern_in_fix` 8 条不上升为发现。

\noindent\textbf{Motivation:}
RQ1 showed that most closures are silence. Asking for the root cause of “failed AI PRs” on all 509 closures would rediscover abandonment and name it incompetence. The useful failure set is the subset in which a human or a blocking signal left a mark. Within that subset we ask which concerns dominate, and how they sit on evidence, process, and in-PR repair.

\noindent\textbf{Approach:}
We do not use all 509 closed PRs as the failure sample. We keep closures that have a formal review, a blocking flag, \texttt{CHANGES\_REQUESTED}, or a review-comment bucket other than \code{no\_review\_text}. This subset has 195 PRs (38.3\% of closed). We then read the three \code{boundary\_tag} values as analytic non-code capabilities. Antipattern labels are a negative control.

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
\textbf{After silence is removed, reviewed failures look like ordinary engineering failures; agent-specific fragility appears at evidence, process, and repair.}
On the full closed set, 329 of 509 PRs (64.6\%) have \code{no\_review\_text}. ### 6.4 RQ4: How to improve the performance of AI-generated PRs?

**书写思路（中文）。** 开篇钉死：这里的 improve 指改善这些性能 PR 的结局/合入过程，不是让生成代码跑得更快。对照用 RQ 报告。明确不支持“给所有 PR 加 benchmark”。

**配图意见。** boundary 计数 + 合并率（封面图）；`optimization_layer`（$n\ge 20$）；merged vs closed 维度表；快/慢路径示意可放 Discussion。

#### English draft

\noindent\textbf{Motivation:}
The first three questions describe a landscape, a fast success regime, and a reviewed-failure regime. RQ4 asks which dimensions separate the two ends, whether capability-boundary tags move with the outcome, and what can be changed without misunderstanding the fast path. Here, “improve the performance of AI-generated PRs” means improving the outcomes of these performance PRs, not making generated functions faster on a benchmark.

\noindent\textbf{Approach:}
We compare merged and closed PRs on lifetime, churn, interaction, materials, \code{perf\_focus}, and \code{optimization\_layer} (layers with terminal $n\ge 20$). We then overlay \code{boundary\_tag}. All contrasts are descriptive. An intervention is proposed only when it matches the path that produced the contrast.

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
\textbf{Merged and closed PRs separate first by speed and boundary type, not by who attached the richer table.}
Merged PRs have a median lifetime of 0.077 hours and a 78.7\% fast-merge share; closed PRs have a median lifetime of 23.6 hours and no fast merge. Median churn is 141 versus 172 lines; the $\le 100$-line bin is healthiest (61.6\%), but large PRs still merge about half the time. Interaction is higher on the closed side. Materials run against the claim that more benchmarks yield more merges: closed PRs more often carry numeric claims (24.8\% vs 13.0\%) and benchmark tables (6.3\% vs 2.4\%). Merged \code{perf\_focus} values more often include constant folding and compiler optimization; closed values more often include bundle-size reduction, build performance, and code splitting. Among layers with $n\ge 20$, compiler backend (26, 84.6\%) and compiler (45, 80.0\%) sit high; \code{runtime\_vm} sits at 24.1\% (29). These layer cells are small and are not treated as a ranking of model skill.

Capability boundaries move with the outcome. Technical-stack PRs have a terminal merge rate of 79.6\%; process PRs 35.7\%; evidence-required PRs 12.5\%. We state this as stratification, not as a causal graph. Process drop-off may be maintainer attention rather than an agent that cannot write a patch. Evidence drop-off may be a project standard that the current agent loop does not treat as a first-class task.

Those contrasts imply two programs, not one.

\textit{Program A, the fast path (RQ1.2, RQ2).} Keep patches atomic and inside a routine stack. Spend the PR body on what changed and why it is safe. Do not place unsolicited optimizations on a deep-review queue by default. Do not enforce a 100-line rule.

\textit{Program B, the slow path (RQ1.3, RQ3).} When a change will be reviewed in depth, attach a before/after table and reproduction steps that meet an evidence standard. Treat \texttt{CHANGES\_REQUESTED} as a first-class agent task: 82 of 148 in-PR fixes are already labeled human-led or human-requested. Declare risk early for \code{runtime\_vm}, wide control-flow edits, and bundle-size work, or split the evidence commit from the code commit. For abandonment, a stale bot that asks for one maintainer sentence is better aligned with the mechanism partition than a bot that only closes.

We do not support the claim that adding a benchmark to every performance PR would raise merge rate. That claim points in the opposite direction of the material signals.

\begin{center}
\begin{tcolorbox}
\textbf{Finding 4.} Merged and closed PRs separate first by lifetime and boundary type (79.6\% vs 35.7\% vs 12.5\%); adding a benchmark to every performance PR is not supported by the data.
\end{tcolorbox}
\end{center}

#### 中文翻译

**动机。** 前三问分别描述版图、快成功与被审失败。RQ4 问哪些维度分开两端、边界标签是否与结果同向，以及在不误解快路径的前提下能改什么。此处 “improve the performance” 指改善这些性能 PR 的结局，不是让生成函数在基准上更快。

**方法。** 在寿命、变更、互动、材料、`perf_focus` 与 $n\ge 20$ 的层面对照，再叠 `boundary_tag`。对照都是描述性的。

**结果。合入与关闭首先由速度和边界类型分开，而不是由谁附了更完整的表。** 寿命、规模、材料方向与 RQ 报告一致。`compiler_backend` 84.6%（n=26），`runtime_vm` 24.1%（n=29），小样本不作能力排名。边界：79.6% / 35.7% / 12.5%，是分层不是因果图。

方案 A 快路径：原子补丁、常规栈、降低注意力成本、不要立法 <100 行。方案 B 慢路径：对准证据标准、把 CHANGES_REQUESTED 当一等任务、拆分高风险改动、stale 要一句话而不是直接关。不支持给每条 PR 加 benchmark。

**Finding 4.** 合入与关闭首先由寿命和边界类型分开（79.6% vs 35.7% vs 12.5%）；给每条性能 PR 加 benchmark 不被数据支持。

### English draft

In this section, we discuss four implications of the study.

\textbf{Merge rate is the wrong headline for performance work.}
A terminal merge rate of 56.9\% (671/1,180) can be quoted as modest or as already useful. Both quotes are compatible with the average, and neither describes the process. Performance engineering is a claim-and-check discipline. Most merged PRs in this corpus never entered the check. Most closed PRs never entered an argument. Evaluations that publish only merge rate will reward small, quiet patches in repositories that merge quickly, and will penalize harder, more documented work. A performance-relevant agent metric should at least stratify by review presence, lifetime, and whether a numeric claim was requested.

\textbf{Two regimes should not share one playbook.}
The fast path is already an equilibrium: routine technical-stack edits, read quickly, merged before a conversation starts. The reviewed path is a different job: correctness under project abstractions, design fit, CI, and sometimes a measurement standard. Advice that averages the two taxes the path that currently produces most merges, and does not obviously rescue PRs that die of silence. Tooling should classify the PR before it applies a single checklist.

\textbf{Evidence production is part of the agent loop, not a courtesy.}
When the boundary tag is \code{evidence\_required}, the terminal merge rate is 12.5\% (4/32), and sufficient materials are 2.1\% of the corpus. Closed PRs already contain more numeric claims than merged PRs. The failure is meeting a checkable standard when a human asks. An agent loop that can edit code but cannot produce a before/after table, a reproduction command, and a refusal to fabricate a benchmark is incomplete for performance work.

\textbf{Agent differences are real in the table and unidentified as model skill.}
OpenAI Codex and Devin differ by more than a factor of two in terminal merge rate (71.9\% vs 32.4\%). We do not narrate that gap as “model A is stronger than model B.” The identities travel with task shape, host repositories, and patch size. A fair comparison would hold the repository and the performance task fixed and randomize the agent—an experiment this observational corpus cannot host. The practical implication is narrower: a dashboard that reports one agent-wide merge rate hides the largest operational variation in the table.

### 中文翻译

本节讨论四点含义。

**合并率是性能工作的错误头条。** 56.9%（671/1,180）既可以写成一般，也可以写成已经有用；两种写法都与平均数兼容，都没有描述过程。评测至少应按是否有审查、寿命、以及是否被要求给出数字来分层。

**书写思路（中文）。** 三块都写具体限制。本版数字已锁在 RQ 报告；不要再把 669/511 写成内部效度主故事。可写：主表 refresh 与分析标签曾有过 2 条漂移，本文以 RQ 报告快照为准。

### English draft

In this section, we discuss threats to the validity of our study.

\textbf{External validity.}
The corpus is GitHub-visible agent PRs from an AIDev-expanded ecosystem, dominated by OpenAI Codex (639/1,219) and by public repositories that host agents. Private monorepos, internal performance review boards, and agents that never open a PR are out of scope. Findings about fast merge may not travel to projects that disable auto-merge or require two reviewers. We mitigate this by reporting per-agent counts and by not treating cells with $n<30$ as product rankings.

\textbf{Internal validity.}
Mechanism groups (real rejection versus silent abandonment) coarsen long-tailed \code{outcome\_reason} strings. Detection method is unknown for 64.5\% of PRs, so “maintainers mostly read code” is a statement about the observable subset. Who performed an in-PR fix is a text heuristic. A July 2026 GitHub refresh snapshot recorded 669 merged and 511 closed PRs; this paper locks outcomes to the official RQ analysis (671/509/39). We publish the schema and few-shot set so that the labels can be audited.

\textbf{Construct validity.}
Merge is not quality. Close is not a written rejection. A performance PR is a PR we classified as performance-related, not a PR that improved a measured metric. \code{boundary\_tag} is an analytic label, not a measurement of cognitive capability. We never execute the patches, so we cannot confirm that a merged change is faster. The construct we claim is the public process that performance-oriented agent PRs undergo.

## 9. Related work（`RelatedWork.tex`）

**书写思路（中文）。** 四块，每块以 *Different from / In comparison* 收束。`ref.bib` 需补实引。

### English draft

In this section, we discuss research related to this work.

### Autonomous coding agents

A growing literature studies autonomous coding agents as authors of pull requests rather than as autocomplete engines. Dataset papers in the AIDev family document that agents already submit large volumes of PRs and that acceptance is uneven across products and task types. Complementary qualitative studies describe how developers supervise agents and negotiate trust. Different from studies that treat merge or rejection rate as the dependent variable, we restrict the unit to performance-oriented PRs and split those rates into mechanisms.

### LLM-generated code quality, including performance

Empirical work on GitHub Copilot and later code models has measured functional correctness, security smells, and the runtime performance of generated functions on controlled benchmarks. The EMSE study on assessing the performance of AI-generated code asks whether AI help is free at the performance layer, but its objects are generated solutions on programming tasks. In comparison, a snippet that is slower on a benchmark is a different failure from a maintainer who asks for a real benchmark on a living system.

### Software performance in the agent era

Performance-engineering research has studied how to detect regressions when workloads drift, including black-box models that relate runtime activities to resource use. Those papers treat the system as the object and the workload as the confounder. Different from that line of work, we do not treat an autonomous agent as a detector. We treat it as the author of the change under review. The bottleneck we observe is often the missing ticket, the missing number, and the missing reviewer.

### Pull-request acceptance and abandonment

Classic mining studies of human PRs already show that size, latency, review attention, and stale closure shape acceptance. Silent abandonment is not an AI invention. What changes in our corpus is who opens the work: 1,004 of 1,219 PRs (82.4\%) have no linked issue. In comparison, we use the human-PR literature as a prior over process, and we use the agent identity to explain why a low-attention equilibrium can still produce a 56.9\% terminal merge rate.

四块分别对照：智能体 PR 文献（他们报合并率，我们拆机制）；函数级 LLM 性能（单位是 snippet，我们是 PR 过程）；黑盒/现场性能工程（对象是系统，我们的对象是智能体作为作者）；人类 PR 接受与遗弃（遗弃不是新现象，但 82.4% 无 issue 改变了谁打开工作）。

## 10. Conclusion（`Conclusions.tex`）

**书写思路（中文）。** 半页。重述已有数字，不新增。

**配图意见。** 无。

### English draft

In this work, we analyzed 1,219 agent-authored performance PRs from 446 repositories. If these PRs are scored by merge rate alone, both successes and failures are misread. The terminal merge rate is 56.9\% (671/1,180), but the average is the least informative fact. Most merges are low-friction and almost unreviewed. Most closes are silent. When a close contains a human argument, the argument is usually about correctness or design. Process and evidence boundaries stratify outcomes from 79.6\% to 12.5\%. Improving this picture is not a single prompt trick and not a universal benchmark requirement. It is a decision about which path a PR is on, and a refusal to call that path a model capability until the process has been seen.
